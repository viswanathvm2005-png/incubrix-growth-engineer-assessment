# Planning a review process for a small video editing team

Freelance editors and two or three person content teams often lose time because approvals happen in scattered chat messages. A lightweight review process fixes this without adding software. Start by listing the stages a video passes through: rough cut, captions, brand check, rights check and final approval.

Assign one named owner to each stage, and write down what that person looks at. For example, the captions owner reads the whole transcript aloud and checks names and numbers. The rights owner confirms that every music track, font and stock clip has a recorded licence.

Set a fixed review window, such as Monday morning for the week's batch, so work does not wait on ad hoc requests. Keep one shared sheet with a row per video: title, source, owner, status and date approved. Anything without an approved date does not get scheduled.

When feedback arrives from clients or collaborators, log it verbatim and decide once a week which changes to apply. Over a month this log shows which mistakes repeat, such as unclear openings or missing links, and which checklist items to add. Teams should only report improvements they measured, using their own before and after turnaround times.
